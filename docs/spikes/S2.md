# Spike S2: per person transcription in one Daily room

Date: 2026-11-15. Card t_e5298af1. Pipecat 1.15.0, daily-python 0.38.0, Deepgram
`nova-3-general` (live websocket).

Question: can ONE Pipecat bot in a Daily room transcribe each participant's audio track
separately, so every transcript line is labeled caller or agent without diarization?

## Verdict

WORKS. Approach: Pipecat's Daily transport per participant audio
(`DailyParams.audio_in_user_tracks=True`) feeding a `ParallelPipeline` with one Deepgram
STT stream per participant. No custom transport code and no diarization.

In 3 of 3 final live runs, every final line carried the correct role (51 of 51 lines)
and no line contained words from the other person's script, including during ~1 s of
cross talk. Four earlier runs while the test was being tuned also had every line
correctly labeled; one of them failed only because the test's word matcher (then
requiring 50% word overlap) could not attribute the misrecognized line "And you just
turned over call me back." to its script. That line was still labeled caller, correctly.
The matcher now accepts any shared word within the time window. Final transcripts arrive
0.32 to 0.61 s after the end of speech (median 0.44 s). The bot uses ~35% of one vCPU and
~155 MB RSS with 2 participants.

Fallbacks from the PRD (a second listen only bot, or diarization) were not needed, so
they were not tested.

## How it works

`bot/agent_assist_bot/listen.py`, entry point `python -m agent_assist_bot.spike_s2 --room-url URL`.

```
DailyTransport(audio_in_user_tracks=True, audio_out_enabled=False).input()
  -> ParallelPipeline(
       [RoleAudioFilter(caller), VADProcessor(Silero), DeepgramSTTService, TranscriptEmitter(caller)],
       [RoleAudioFilter(agent),  VADProcessor(Silero), DeepgramSTTService, TranscriptEmitter(agent)],
     )
```

- With `audio_in_user_tracks=True` (Pipecat's default for Daily) the transport calls
  `set_audio_renderer` per participant on join and pushes `UserAudioRawFrame`s tagged
  with `user_id` = Daily participant id. Nothing is mixed.
- `ParticipantRoles` maps participant id -> role from the Daily `user_name` on
  `on_participant_joined` (spike rule: the name is literally "caller" or "agent"; other
  names are ignored). Removed on `on_participant_left`; the bot ends when no labeled
  participant is left.
- Each branch's `RoleAudioFilter` drops audio that is not its role's, so each Deepgram
  websocket only ever hears one person.
- Each branch has its own Silero VAD so `VADUserStoppedSpeakingFrame` triggers
  Deepgram's `Finalize` for that stream only (faster finals).
- `TranscriptEmitter` turns `TranscriptionFrame` / `InterimTranscriptionFrame` into
  `TranscriptLine{speaker_role, participant_id, text, is_final, ts}`. The spike prints
  final lines to stdout as `S2LINE {json}`; interims are produced but not printed.
- Listen only: no TTS, no LLM, `audio_out_enabled=False`.

## Test

`bot/tests/test_live_s2.py` (`@pytest.mark.live`). Command:
`uv run pytest -m live bot/tests/test_live_s2.py -s` (needs DAILY_API_KEY, DEEPGRAM_API_KEY).

- Creates a temporary room (expires in 540 s, deleted at the end), starts the bot as a
  subprocess, and joins two S1 harness clients named "caller" and "agent".
- 9 scripted turns, alternating. Audio is Deepgram TTS made at test time: caller voice
  `aura-2-arcas-en`, agent voice `aura-2-thalia-en`. The two scripts use different
  words so cross talk would be visible.
- Turn 7 (caller) starts 1.0 s before the agent's turn 6 ends (measured against the last
  voiced audio, not trailing silence). Measured overlap: 0.979 / 0.967 / 0.988 s.
- Each final line is matched to the utterance it came from using only its words and
  arrival time (never its label). The test then asserts:
  1. every line's `speaker_role` matches the role of its `participant_id` (ids taken from
     the harness clients, not from the bot);
  2. every line's words come from an utterance by that same role (no cross talk);
  3. every utterance produced at least one line;
  4. the overlapping pair (agent turn 6, caller turn 7) each got their own line with the
     right role.
- CPU and memory: the test samples the bot's `/proc/<pid>/stat` and `status` every
  0.5 s (`bot/tests/harness/procstats.py`).

Breakdown (separate scratch measurement, 15 s windows, not part of the committed test):

| Condition | Avg CPU | Peak RSS |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Bot alone in the room | 0.2% | 152.6 MB |
| 2 silent participants | 34.9% | 154.3 MB |
| 2 silent participants, Silero VADs replaced with a pass-through | 28.3% | 138.8 MB |

So the cost is per connected track, not per word: receiving and decoding two Opus tracks,
the per 20 ms Python audio callbacks, and streaming to two Deepgram websockets is ~28%
of a core; the two Silero VADs add ~6 points and ~15 MB. Talking vs silent makes no
difference.

## Observations and caveats for the build

- Labels are by construction: a line's role comes from the track it was heard on, so
  accuracy does not depend on the speech model. Speaker swaps are not possible unless the
  participant -> role mapping is wrong.
- Role mapping from `user_name` is a spike shortcut. The build should assign roles from
  the meeting token (`owner` / user_id) or from the call flow (the dial-in leg is the
  caller), not from a display name anyone can set.
- One branch per role. With one caller and one agent this is one stream per person. If
  two people share a role (e.g. a supervisor joins as "agent"), their audio would share
  a Deepgram stream and `user_id` on the lines would flip between them. If that matters,
  build one branch per participant dynamically instead of per role.
- Deepgram splits one turn into several final lines at pauses ("Sure." / "It is
  Harrington." / "Spelled the usual way."). The portal or the assist logic should join
  consecutive same-speaker lines into one turn (e.g. on `speech_final` /
  `UtteranceEnd` or a short gap).
- In 2 of 7 live runs the first word of the agent's first turn ("Thanks") was missing
  ("For waiting."). The same text and voice were used each time and the caller's first word
  was never lost. Likely the first packets of the agent's custom track after it first
  goes active (S1 saw the same for the bot's first word). Not a labeling issue; recheck
  with a real browser and the phone leg in S3/S4.
- CPU: ~35% of a core per 2-person call on this host means a few concurrent calls per
  vCPU. Fine for the PoC; capacity planning belongs with deployment.
- Phone audio (8 kHz SIP leg) is S3. These numbers are WebRTC at 16 kHz.
- Teardown logs one `Mediasoup action close consumer after sfu failed: TaskAborted` from
  daily-core when the harness clients leave. Harmless.
